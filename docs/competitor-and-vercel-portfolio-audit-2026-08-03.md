# rajeevg.com authority and portfolio audit

Date: 3 August 2026

## Executive conclusion

rajeevg.com is now clean, credible, and easy to understand, but it is not yet operating like an authoritative industry publication. The strongest comparable sites do more than present a polished biography and a set of project links. They repeatedly connect four things:

1. a clear area of expertise;
2. original products or methods;
3. a growing body of useful writing;
4. visible evidence that the work is current and used.

The current site has the raw material for this. The missing piece is a stronger public system around it. Rajeev has more working software than the site previously showed, but the writing archive is still small, most projects do not have their own case-study page, and the homepage describes four broad fields without showing which one Rajeev most wants to be known for.

Overall authority score: **74/100**. The site is professionally presentable today. Reaching an 85–90 standard requires clearer subject ownership, deeper project evidence, a useful subscription or feed option, and a consistent publishing rhythm.

## What the strongest sites have in common

### 1. They own a recognisable subject

Simon Willison is strongly associated with practical work on language models, open-source tools, and data software. Gunnar Griese is tightly associated with advanced analytics architecture. DHH and 37signals are associated with a specific way of building and running software companies.

rajeevg.com currently says “AI, data, analytics, and adtech.” That is accurate but broad. A stronger public position is:

> I build practical AI and measurement systems, then publish the evidence behind them.

This still allows local models, product software, analytics, and advertising technology, but gives the reader one idea to remember.

### 2. Their products and writing strengthen each other

The current site often links a project to a live product and source repository, but most projects stop there. The stronger pattern is:

`project page → build account → technical note → reusable method → related project`

Local LLM Lab is a good candidate for this treatment. It already has a live guide, exact model inventory, benchmark evidence, raw results, and a related article. The site should turn that into a complete case study rather than only a card.

### 3. They make current activity visible

Authority is partly accumulated and partly refreshed. A visitor should be able to tell what Rajeev is working on now, what changed recently, and which projects remain maintained.

Add a small “Current work” section with two or three items and dates. Do not add an automated activity stream to the homepage. A short edited list is clearer and more trustworthy.

### 4. They offer a way to return

Simon Willison, Julia Evans, Sriram Krishnan, and Shawn Wang all provide a feed, newsletter, or digest. rajeevg.com currently offers no equivalent.

The first step should be an RSS or Atom feed because it is low maintenance and fits the independent technical audience. Add email only when there is a realistic publishing schedule.

### 5. They curate as well as archive

A chronological list is useful, but a new visitor also needs a route into the best material. Add “Start here” only after there are at least 12–15 strong public pieces. Until then, use three clearly labelled featured guides on the Writing page.

## Interface audit

### Anti-pattern verdict

**Pass, with residual template signals.** The site no longer looks like an unfinished administration interface. Real project imagery, restrained copy, and the editorial homepage prevent it from reading as generic generated work.

Remaining signals:

- Geist, monochrome cards, small badges, and repeated button rows resemble a common Next.js component starter.
- The three featured project cards use the same image-title-badges-buttons formula.
- Technical badges are more visible than project results.
- The headline lists several sectors rather than making one memorable claim.

### Composition verdict

- Desktop width is used well on the homepage and the introductory layout has deliberate balance.
- Wide desktop does not collapse into a narrow central column.
- Whitespace is mostly intentional.
- The Projects page is structurally weaker than the homepage because three identical cards dominate the first section and the remaining projects become a plain list without imagery or evidence.
- No panel is unreasonably tall and narrow on the verified public pages.
- The primary visitor tasks—inspect work, read, and contact—are clear.

### Executive summary

- Critical issues: 0
- High-severity issues: 4
- Medium-severity issues: 5
- Low-severity issues: 3
- Overall interface and authority score: 74/100

The site is usable and credible. The largest gaps concern authority and evidence rather than basic interface quality.

### High-severity findings

#### H1. The public position is too broad

- Location: homepage and About page
- Category: content and positioning
- Impact: visitors can understand the fields Rajeev works in, but may not remember a distinct area of authority.
- Recommendation: lead with practical AI and measurement systems, then use project examples to show the range.

#### H2. Most projects do not have first-party case-study pages

- Location: Projects page
- Category: information architecture
- Impact: important decisions, constraints, outcomes, screenshots, and evidence remain scattered across repositories or are absent from the site.
- Recommendation: create individual pages for the five strongest projects. Each page should cover the user problem, Rajeev's role, the working product, key decisions, evidence, current status, and related writing.

#### H3. There is no subscription or feed route

- Location: Writing page and site metadata
- Category: audience retention
- Impact: interested readers have no simple way to return when new work is published.
- Recommendation: add RSS or Atom first, expose it in the Writing page and document metadata, and only add email when a repeatable publishing schedule exists.

#### H4. Project proof is inconsistent

- Location: project data and cards
- Category: credibility
- Impact: the portfolio explains what each product does, but rarely states whether it is maintained, when it was last updated, who used it, or what measurable result it produced.
- Recommendation: add `status`, `lastUpdated`, `evidence`, and `outcome` fields. Show one proof point, not another row of technology badges.

### Medium-severity findings

#### M1. The writing archive is too small and uneven for category leadership

Ten public post files exist, with most published in March 2026. The August Local Qwen article is strong, but the current pattern does not yet show a reliable publishing habit.

Recommendation: publish one substantial first-hand piece every two to four weeks and one shorter technical note between major articles.

#### M2. The site lacks a concise current-work section

Recommendation: add a manually edited “Current work” block on About or the homepage with no more than three dated items.

#### M3. Structured data covers articles but not the profile and software portfolio

Recommendation: add `ProfilePage` and `Person` data to About, and `SoftwareApplication` or `CreativeWork` data to individual project pages when those pages exist.

#### M4. The About page repeats the homepage more than it proves experience

Recommendation: replace one explanatory paragraph with a compact proof list: current focus, representative products, teaching or speaking, relevant operating experience, and location.

#### M5. The Projects page gives private-source products less context than public-source products

Recommendation: never manufacture a GitHub link. Give private-source products a proper case study and live-product link, and state only that source is not public when that information helps the visitor.

### Low-severity findings

#### L1. The typography and component styling are safe rather than distinctive

Recommendation: keep the restrained editorial direction, but introduce one more distinctive editorial type treatment for major headings and project case-study titles.

#### L2. Pure white and near-black tokens make the visual identity generic

Recommendation: tint the neutral palette slightly and use a single restrained brand colour connected to the strongest project imagery.

#### L3. Technology badges receive too much repeated emphasis

Recommendation: show the full stack on project detail pages and reduce cards to two or three relevant technologies plus one evidence point.

### Positive findings

- The navigation is concise and audience-facing.
- The homepage has clear hierarchy and useful first-viewport actions.
- Desktop, wide desktop, tablet, and mobile layouts have already passed browser acceptance checks.
- Article pages have proper metadata and article structured data.
- Public projects use real screenshots rather than decorative illustrations.
- The writing is plain spoken and generally avoids promotional language.
- Private dashboards and retired analytics routes are no longer exposed as main visitor experiences.
